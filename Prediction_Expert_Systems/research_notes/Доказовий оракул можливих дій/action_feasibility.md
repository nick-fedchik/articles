# Evidence-grounded action feasibility for an expert-system oracle

## Semantics and system boundary

The oracle should answer “Which actions are supported by this corpus, under which stated conditions?”—not “What will a person probably do?” Its output is an evidence-indexed inventory, not a behavioral forecast or an instruction to act. Treating every imperative sentence as a currently available action would conflate quotations, proposals, obligations, permissions, and operational capability.

Represent each normalized action candidate as a record such as
`(action, actor, object, modality, conditions, valid-time, jurisdiction/scope, source-span, document/revision, polarity, attribution)`. Preserve the exact supporting passage and whether it is an authorial statement, quotation, reported claim, hypothetical, or negation. Separate five judgments: **extracted** (the text mentions an action); **documentary status** (the source requests, requires, permits, prohibits, or merely describes it); **preconditions** (what facts must hold); **feasibility** (whether capability/resources/time are evidenced); and **recommendation/ranking** (an optional policy objective). None entails the next. Execution is outside the oracle: a separately authorized controller would need fresh state checks and its own approval and safety controls.

“Feasible” must therefore be qualified. A document can support “procedure X permits actor A to do Y if C,” but that does not establish that C is true, the actor has organizational authority, the act is physically possible now, or no higher-priority rule prohibits it. Do not silently turn corpus absence into a negative fact. Use explicit three-valued evidence (`supported`, `refuted`, `unknown`), with `contested` where incompatible supported claims remain. A corpus-relative **evidence-supported feasible** label should require a cited action, satisfied and current preconditions, evidence of relevant capability/resources and actor authority, and no undefeated applicable prohibition; otherwise return the documented action with unresolved proof obligations, not a binary “possible.”

## What established formalisms contribute

STRIPS models an action schema through preconditions and add/delete effects over a state; PDDL standardizes domain/problem descriptions and later PDDL 2.1 adds numeric fluents, durative actions, temporal invariants, and concurrency. These formalisms support planning from a specified state and goal, not interpreting document authority or measuring whether the encoded state is true. Their conventional closed-world planning state must not be mistaken for an evidence corpus where missing claims are unknown. [Fikes and Nilsson, “STRIPS”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(71)90010-5); [Fox and Long, “PDDL2.1”](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1129). **Technical research**, not normative policy.

In situation calculus, actions transform situations and a `Poss(a,s)` predicate states when an action is executable; successor-state axioms specify what changes and what persists. This cleanly separates an action’s executability conditions from its effects, but the axioms remain model assumptions: they do not prove real-world capability, legal permission, or source reliability. [Reiter, *Knowledge in Action*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262182311/knowledge-in-action/). Allen-style interval reasoning and the W3C OWL-Time vocabulary can represent interval ordering, duration, and dates; use these to test whether evidence is in force at the query time, while keeping publication time distinct from validity time. [W3C OWL-Time](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl-time/), a **technical Recommendation**.

Deontic representations distinguish obligation, permission, and prohibition; defeasible rules allow exceptions and priorities. Argumentation frameworks model claims as arguments and attacks, then define accepted arguments under a selected semantics. They provide a principled way to expose conflicts, but the chosen priority, scope, and acceptance policy are system choices, not facts discovered by majority vote. A deontic argumentation proposal combines these ideas; it is **technical research**, not a legal standard. [Governatori, Rotolo, and Riveret, “A Deontic Argumentation Framework Based on Deontic Defeasible Logic”](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-03098-8_33); [Dung, “On the acceptability of arguments…”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(94)00041-X).

Attach provenance to every assertion: source identity, author/issuer, quotation status, extraction location, revision, and derivation. W3C PROV-O supplies a standard vocabulary for entities, activities, agents, and derivation; it records lineage but does not certify truth or authority. [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/REC-prov-o-20130430/), a **W3C Recommendation**. For standards text, RFC 8174 clarifies that RFC 2119 terms have defined requirement force only when uppercase; ordinary lowercase “must” or “may” is not automatically normative. That rule applies to IETF specifications, not arbitrary documents, so it is a useful source-specific parsing rule rather than a universal linguistic classifier. [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174.html), an IETF **Best Current Practice**.

## Abstention and a testable candidate set

Return conditional and quoted passages as candidates with their status preserved, never as unconditional permissions. Missing facts leave preconditions unknown; contradictions yield `contested`; stale or superseded revisions are ineligible unless the query explicitly asks for historical status. A newer timestamp alone is not proof of supersession: require an explicit revision relation or configured authority rule. If valid-time, actor, jurisdiction, or quotation scope cannot be resolved, abstain from the stronger permission/feasibility judgment and show what evidence is missing. Do not merge a “not documented” result with “prohibited.”

Define `C(D,q)` as all normalized action mentions in corpus `D` relevant to query `q`, each paired with provenance and the five judgments above. Test the set against expert-annotated action spans and labels. Report span/action precision and recall, modality/condition F1, evidence-link accuracy, false-positive rate for unconditional permission, contradiction-detection recall, abstention rate, and the proportion of abstentions later adjudicated resolvable. These are evaluation metrics, not guarantees of completeness; publish corpus and annotation scope.

Three falsifiable experiments:
1. **Quotation and modality challenge:** create matched passages that state, quote, deny, request, permit, and condition the same action. Prediction: the oracle extracts the same underlying action where appropriate, but does not label quoted/denied/conditional mentions as unconditional current permission. Measure false-positive permission rate and modality F1 against blinded annotators.
2. **Revision and time challenge:** provide paired documents with explicit supersession, overlapping effective intervals, and a newer-but-unrelated revision. Prediction: only explicit applicable supersession changes the current answer; the unrelated newer revision must not silently win. Measure status accuracy and abstention on unresolved validity.
3. **Precondition and conflict challenge:** vary one precondition among supported, contradicted, and absent, and add an applicable prohibition or higher-priority exception. Prediction: only the supported case can advance to evidence-supported feasibility; contradiction or unresolved conflict yields `contested`/abstention, not majority-vote permission. Measure decision accuracy and calibration by evidence state.

These are recommended acceptance tests, not claims that any formalism guarantees correct extraction or complete knowledge of the world. The strongest defensible oracle answer is a traceable, bounded statement about what the supplied evidence supports at a specified time and scope.
